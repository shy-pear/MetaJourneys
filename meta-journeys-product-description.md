# Meta Journeys — Product Description & Problem Statement

## In one line

Journeys is a new Instagram format for documenting progress and life chapters over time —
turning Meta from the place people discover interests into the place they develop them.

---

## The problem

Meta wins discovery and loses progression.

Instagram Reels manufactures aspiration at enormous scale. Every day, millions of people
watch something — a golf swing, a sourdough loaf, a marathon finish, a renovated kitchen —
and feel the pull of "I want to do that." But at the exact moment that intent forms, Meta has
nothing to catch it. Users leave for Google, YouTube, Reddit, and increasingly AI assistants
to figure out how to actually start. Meta owns the attention but not the action, and as AI
captures more of these high-intent moments, that gap becomes a strategic threat.

Underneath that is a second, quieter problem. Instagram's main grid has become a performance.
Curation anxiety — the pressure to post only polished, finished, on-brand content — has
suppressed authentic, in-progress sharing and pushed it into Stories, DMs, and Close Friends.
People won't post "day one, I'm bad at this" to a feed built for highlights. Yet that
in-progress content is exactly what an inspired user wants to create and what other beginners
want to see. The behavior already exists in fragments — recurring Stories, Highlights,
progress posts — but Meta has never given it a home.

So the problem Journeys solves is twofold: the inspiration-to-action leak that sends
high-intent users off-platform, and the absence of a low-pressure format where progress and
life chapters can be shared without the weight of the curated grid.

---

## The product

Journeys is a persistent, timeline-based format for documenting something as it unfolds.

If Stories answer "what's happening today?" and your profile answers "who am I?", Journeys
answer "who am I becoming?" Crucially, that question is broad on purpose. A Journey can be a
skill or goal — marathon training, learning golf, building a startup, improving as a
photographer — or it can be a life chapter with no finish line at all: a pregnancy, a year of
travel, a home renovation, a child's first years. Anchoring the format to documentation rather
than goal-achievement is what gives it mass reach instead of confining it to a
self-improvement niche.

You start a Journey by giving it a theme and an optional expected timeline. You add updates
whenever you like — story-style posts of a photo and some text, with an optional longer
description, and optionally short video. Updates are quick and informal by design; no new
posting skills are required. The timeline is editable at any time, and you can choose to
receive gentle reminders to post, though reminders are off by default and offered only at
creation so the feature never nags.

The single most important design decision is that Journey posts live off the main grid. They
are grouped into Journey entries on your profile — opened and tapped through like Stories —
and never clutter your curated feed. This is what makes imperfect, in-progress content
postable: sharing a beginner attempt carries none of the identity weight of posting it to your
permanent grid. It solves the authenticity problem structurally, with no privacy settings
required.

---

## How it works, end to end

A user discovers an interest through Reels or Explore. When they decide to act, an optional,
unbranded helper — surfaced simply as "feeling stuck?" or "looking for inspiration?" — meets
them where they are and points them toward creators, content, communities, businesses, and
products that help them start. If they begin documenting, that same helper periodically asks
what their next step is and tailors what it surfaces to their stage.

The first update a person posts triggers a one-time, low-friction invitation to their
followers: "started a Marathon journey — follow along?" From then on, ongoing updates reach
only the people who opted in, appearing in their feed as native posts rather than as
skippable "made an update" notifications. Anyone — follower or not — can follow along with a
Journey without following the creator, which lowers the social cost of committing to a story
and gives Meta a sharp, specific interest signal. High-performing Journeys are occasionally
resurfaced to followers who haven't opted in, as a second-chance invitation.

Viewers like and comment on individual posts, and the Journey displays aggregate likes and
comments across its whole arc, rewarding the narrative rather than any single post. Creators
can see who has viewed their Journey.

A Journey ends in one of three ways. It can be paused or archived — removed from feeds without
fanfare or shame, so a stalled effort never becomes a public monument to giving up — or it can
be completed. Completion is treated as the emotional payoff of the entire format: Meta
auto-assembles a celebratory summary of the arc (the span, the milestones, the people who
followed along), which the user can customize and caption before sharing. A finished Journey
is both a trophy and the single best recruitment tool the product has, because watching a
friend's completed "couch to marathon" arc is precisely what makes the next person start one.

---

## The guidance layer

The recommendation engine is Meta AI, surfaced contextually rather than as a separate brand —
so there is no new name to learn, and the same assistant extends naturally to future surfaces.
It is deliberately quiet, invoked only when a user wants help via the "feeling stuck?" prompt.
It begins with organic recommendations only; sponsored recommendations are introduced later,
always clearly labeled, and only after the engine has demonstrably proven useful. A simple
"was this helpful?" control lets users tune what they see and visibly shapes future
suggestions, building the trust that makes the eventual sponsored recommendations worth
clicking.

---

## Discovery and distribution

Public Journeys are discoverable beyond a creator's own followers. On Explore and in topic
search, they appear as distinct progress cards — a cover, a title, and a stage line such as
"Marathon training · week 6 of 16 · 340 following along" — so that someone can find and join a
story already in motion, and so a beginner can be found by other people at the same stage.
This interest-matched discovery is what prevents Journeys from being posted into an empty room.

Video Journey posts can additionally flow into Reels carrying a "view full journey?" button,
converting passive scrollers into followers. This is opt-in per post and limited to video, so
Reels keeps its existing format and quality. Over time, Reels can also host a "try this out?"
entry point that lets Meta AI recommend a starting point and spin up a new Journey directly.

---

## Audience model

Journeys are either public or followers-only. The format intentionally forgoes more granular
privacy tiers in favor of simplicity, since the off-grid container already removes most of the
exposure concern. Public Journeys can surface on Explore and in Reels; followers-only Journeys
reach only the people who follow the creator and opt in. This is a deliberate scoping choice:
Journeys is a social, semi-public format, not a private journal.

---

## How it makes money

In the near term, Journeys generates high-intent signals that improve ad targeting and
relevance across the platform. In the mid term, the guidance layer carries sponsored,
clearly-labeled recommendations matched to where a user is in their Journey — lessons and gear
for a golf Journey, venues and vendors for a wedding, hotels and experiences for travel.
Longer term, the format opens into commerce, creator partnerships, affiliate relationships,
premium coaching, and the hardware ecosystem.

---

## The long-term vision

Journeys is one stage in a larger flywheel: Reels create inspiration, the guidance layer turns
inspiration into action, Journeys turn action into documented progress, and — eventually, and
only with explicit opt-in — Meta's glasses turn progress into real-world coaching. The glasses
can keep someone on track, support them toward a goal, and help capture the moments worth
sharing, feeding content back into the Journey and closing the loop: glasses capture, the
Journey holds, the social graph rewards, the AI guides, the glasses coach.

The strategic point is that no competitor can assemble all four pieces. TikTok has discovery
but no persistent real-identity graph and an algorithm tuned for watch time, not user growth.
AI assistants have guidance but no social context or creator ecosystem. Only Meta holds the
social graph, the creators, the cross-app surfaces — WhatsApp for accountability, Facebook as a
natural second home for life-documentation Journeys — and a hardware roadmap to extend coaching
into the real world.

Meta does not need to build a better chatbot. It needs to strengthen what only Meta has:
discovery, identity, social motivation, communities, and creators. Journeys is how Meta evolves
from the world's largest platform for discovering interests into the world's largest platform
for developing them.
