# Meta Journeys — Launch Marketing Plan

A launch plan built around the product's actual challenge: not awareness, but overcoming an
entrenched default (Stories → Highlights) and making the act of sharing imperfect, in-progress
content feel aspirational instead of exposing.

---

## The objective and the real challenge

The objective is not "tell people Journeys exists." Reach is the easy part when you own Instagram.
The objective is to **make starting a Journey feel desirable and safe** for a normal user — to pull
them off the comfortable Highlights/Stories default and get them to publicly document something
unfinished.

Two truths shape every decision below:

1. **The enemy is a default, not a competitor.** People already document progress through Stories and
   group it in Highlights. Marketing has to reframe that habit as the *first rung* of something better,
   not fight it.
2. **The barrier is emotional, not functional.** Posting "day one, I'm bad at this" cuts against
   Instagram's curated, aspirational culture. The campaign's central job is to make beginning —
   visibly, imperfectly — feel brave and aspirational rather than embarrassing.

If the marketing solves those two, the product's own loop (cheering, follow-along, completion) takes
over. If it doesn't, no amount of reach matters.

---

## Positioning & narrative

**The reframe (internal strategic line):** Journeys is not a new format to learn — it's the natural
next step for something two billion people already do. We are not teaching a behavior; we are
upgrading one.

**The promise to users:** Get recognized for what you're working toward — not just what you've already
achieved. Journeys turns your progress into something people can follow, cheer, and find.

**What we will NOT say:** "A better way to group your content by theme." That framing loses to
Highlights on simplicity and misses the point. We sell distribution, progress, guidance, and being
*seen* — never organization.

**The one-line positioning:**
> Stories show what's happening today. Your profile shows who you are. **Journeys show who you're
> becoming.**

---

## Messaging pillars

Three pillars, each tied to a real product truth and a real user motivation:

1. **Progress, not perfection.** Your grid is your highlight reel; your Journey is the real story.
   Speaks directly to authenticity-fatigue and removes the curation-anxiety barrier.
2. **You don't have to figure it out alone.** Meta AI helps you find the next step — creators,
   communities, the right gear, what to do at your stage. Speaks to the inspiration-to-action problem
   that sends people off-platform.
3. **Someone's rooting for you.** Followers can follow along, cheer each update, and celebrate the
   finish. Speaks to social motivation and accountability — the thing that makes progress stick.

---

## The hero campaign: "Everyone Starts at Day One"

A single, ownable cultural idea that does the emotional heavy lifting.

**The concept:** Pair people's polished, impressive "now" with their humble, awkward beginning — the
marathoner who couldn't run a mile, the photographer's first blurry photo, the founder's messy first
prototype, the parent's first overwhelmed week. The truth it lands: *everything impressive started
somewhere unglamorous, and that somewhere is worth sharing.*

**Why it works:** it reframes the exact thing people are afraid of (posting imperfect beginnings) as
the most relatable, aspirational, human thing they can do. It attacks the curation-anxiety barrier head
on, it implicitly contrasts a living Journey against the static, perfected profile grid, and it's
endlessly extensible across every category and every creator.

**The call to action:** "Start your Day One." Every piece of the campaign drives to the simple,
low-friction act of starting a Journey with a first post — or converting an existing Highlight into one.

---

## Beachhead audiences — launch category by category

Do not launch broadly. Concentrate on communities where progress-sharing is already culturally normal,
milestones are clear, and content already exists — so the format lands where it's least scary first.

- **Wave 1 (high-accountability, clear milestones, already share publicly):** marathon/running, fitness
  transformations, sobriety, language learning.
- **Wave 2 (strong creator ecosystems, skill-based):** golf and skill sports, photography, cooking,
  music, entrepreneurship.
- **Wave 3 (high-emotion, high-ARPU life documentation):** travel, parenting, weddings, home renovation.

Each wave gets its own creator campaign and its own creative, so the message always arrives inside a
community that already values the behavior — never as a generic broadcast.

---

## The engine: the "Founding Journeys" creator program

Creators are not an amplification channel bolted on at the end — they are the launch itself. The
product's norms are set by its earliest content, so we control that content by seeding it.

**Founding Journeys cohort:** recruit a curated group of creators across every beachhead category to
begin a *real* Journey publicly, from scratch, starting at launch — and document it honestly, day one
included. Their Journeys become the reference examples of "what good looks like" and the first thing new
users see.

**What we ask of them, and what they get:** not an ad read, but participation — start a genuine
Journey, post updates on their real cadence, and invite their audience to follow along. Authenticity is
the asset; a polished sponsored post would undercut the entire "progress not perfection" message.

**The incentive — amplification.** Instagram's side of the deal is distribution: Founding Journeys are
actively amplified to relevant, interest-matched audiences beyond the creator's own followers. This is
the most valuable thing Meta can offer and it costs nothing Meta isn't already doing — it's a stronger
pull than a flat fee, it self-selects for creators who want reach (and will therefore post
consistently), and it directly seeds the discovery the whole launch depends on. The clearer the
exemplary Journey, the more reach it earns.

**The two jobs this does at once:**
- Sets the cultural default (honest, in-progress, imperfect is celebrated).
- Turns each creator's community into a ready-made on-ramp — their followers are exactly the
  interest-matched audience a new Journey needs to avoid posting into a void.

**Tiering:** a small number of marquee creators for the cultural moment and PR, a broad mid-tier across
niches for category penetration, and an open "anyone can be a founding member" layer so the program
doesn't feel closed.

## Channel plan

**Owned (Meta's own surfaces — the biggest advantage). This is the complete launch set; notably, no
Reels entry point of any kind — that is a later-phase move the roadmap has to earn first:**
- An Explore feature shelf for Journeys, surfacing exemplary and interest-matched Journeys.
- Feed placement: journey updates and the follow-along invitation card appearing natively in feed.
- The Highlight-to-Journey conversion prompt — the single highest-leverage owned tactic, because it
  reaches people who have *already demonstrated the behavior* and offers a one-tap upgrade.
- The in-product act of starting and posting your own Journey (the launch takeover moment that
  introduces the feature and walks a user into a first post).
- Lifecycle notifications — gentle nudges, strictly limited to users clearly inside a specific
  beachhead community, never broadcast.

**Earned (the cultural moment):**
- PR around the "Day One" idea and the Founding Journeys cohort — a story about authenticity and the
  end of the perfect grid, not a feature announcement.
- Creator-led organic reach as the cohort invites their communities.
- User-generated momentum: completed Journeys (the Wrapped-style recaps) are inherently shareable and
  become earned media on their own.

**Paid:**
- Tightly targeted to beachhead interest communities and to high-intent signals (people who repeatedly
  watch a category's Reels). Not a broad awareness buy.
- Retargeting completed-Journey recaps as proof-driven recruitment for the next cohort.

## Launch sequence (mapped to the rollout roadmap)

**Pre-launch (Phase 0 — seed quietly):** recruit and onboard the Founding Journeys cohort; let them
build real Journeys in the closed beta so there is a body of exemplary content *before* the public
ever arrives. No broad noise yet.

**Launch moment (start of Phase 1):** the "Everyone Starts at Day One" campaign goes live
simultaneously with the cohort's public Journeys and the Highlight-conversion prompt. Concentrate the
splash on Wave 1 categories.

**Sustain (through Phase 1):** roll out Wave 2 and Wave 3 creator campaigns category by category;
begin surfacing the first *completed* Journeys as proof and as the next wave of creative.

**Compounding (Phase 2+):** completed-Journey recaps become the primary, self-renewing marketing asset.
Each later surface (WhatsApp, Reels, Facebook, Glasses) gets its own category-matched creator push
rather than a generic relaunch — life-event creators lead Facebook, skill creators lead the Reels
"try this out?" moment.

---

## Cold-start tactics — making the recognition promise true

The marketing promises recognition; the product has to deliver it or the promise curdles into
disappointment. These tactics protect against the void:

- **Lead with Highlight conversion** so launch-day Journeys arrive already populated, not blank.
- **Route every creator campaign through interest communities** so a beginner's first update reaches
  people who actually care about that topic.
- **Lean on niche Explore discovery in messaging** — "find your people" — so small accounts experience
  real engagement, not silence.
- **Make completion a shareable event** so finishing a Journey recruits the next person.

---

## How we'll measure marketing success

Awareness metrics are necessary but not sufficient. The honest gate is whether marketing-driven users
*behave like real users.*

- **Awareness:** campaign reach, brand-lift on the core message, earned-media volume and sentiment.
- **Activation:** Journey starts attributable to the campaign; Highlight-to-Journey conversion rate.
- **Creator efficacy:** follower → follow-along conversion *from creator audiences*; share of new
  Journeys attributable to creator campaigns vs. organic discovery.
- **The honest gate:** do campaign-sourced Journey-starters hit the product's north-star retention
  (a third update within 30 days, by follower segment)? If acquired users don't retain, the format
  isn't ready to advertise harder — fix the product before spending more on reach.

---

## Risks the marketing must actively avoid

- **Clout drift.** Over-indexing on "go viral" attracts performers and pushes the culture back toward
  the polished grid Journeys was meant to escape. Always frame recognition as being *seen for showing
  up and making progress* — never as follower growth.
- **A hollow recognition promise.** Don't promise discovery the product can't yet deliver for small
  accounts. Marketing and the discovery engine have to ship in lockstep.
- **Highlights confusion.** Never message Journeys as "organize your content." Lead with progress,
  guidance, and being seen — the things Highlights structurally cannot do — so the distinction is
  obvious and the new behavior is clearly worth it.
- **Over-polishing.** The campaign itself must look authentic, not glossy. A slick ad for "post your
  imperfect beginnings" is self-defeating; the creative should feel as human as the behavior it asks for.
